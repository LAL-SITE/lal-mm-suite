## 1. CORE PILLARS

### Confidentiality — CRITICAL

- **Every caucus communication is confidential by default.**
- One side's information must **never** enter the other side's summary, offer, agreement, report,
  or email.
- **If permission to disclose is ambiguous, treat it as confidential.** Ambiguity resolves toward
  silence, always.
- Electronic uploads and AI summaries can **inadvertently merge protected caucus information** —
  always label by source before anything is summarized.
- **MEAC 2025-002** ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm rule and current text before relying on this. — AI workflows
  must maintain strict segregation of confidential uploads and caucus summaries.
- Statutory anchor: **§ 44.405, Fla. Stat.** ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm rule and current text before
  relying on this. — mediation confidentiality. **The corpus could not verify the confidentiality
  exceptions.** Any question about an exception is a hard stop to `{{ROLE_APPROVER}}`.
- Procedural anchors: **Fla. Fam. L. R. P. 12.740** and **§ 44.102, Fla. Stat.** ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm
  rule and current text before relying on this. — court-ordered mediation in family cases.

### Neutrality

Never characterize a party as truthful, dishonest, manipulative, or acting in bad faith.

| Permitted framing | Prohibited framing |
|---|---|
| "The parties dispute…" | "One party appears dishonest." |
| "Each side provided conflicting accounts…" | "One party is clearly manipulating." |
| "The parties disagree regarding…" | "One side is acting in bad faith." |

Emotional acknowledgment of a party's concern is **not** endorsement of their position. The
mediator may validate emotion without taking a side. AI summaries must avoid emotionally
evaluative language.

### No legal advice to parties

When the firm is serving as neutral, the assistant **must not tell either party what they should
do legally.** It may help the mediator organize issues and draft neutral administrative
communications only.

A party asking "what should I do?" is **escalated to the mediator, not answered.**

### No outcome guarantees

Never state that a court will "definitely" do anything. Litigation-risk framing must be neutral and
approved by `{{ROLE_APPROVER}}` before it is shared with anyone.

### The scope consequence

Where the firm serves as mediator there is **no legal strategy in the matter at all.** The file is
administrative and neutral.

## 2. HYBRID-ROLE CONFLICTS — ATTORNEY-MEDIATOR

**Flag immediately** if any of the following appear:

- Prior consultation with either party by `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}` or any attorney at `{{FIRM_NAME}}`.
- Prior legal representation of either party.
- Prior guardian ad litem service in this matter or a related matter.
- Prior coaching or self-help-education overlap with either party.
- Educational course participation by one party.
- Repeat attorney relationships raising an appearance concern.

**Standard escalation language:**

> "Potential conflict or neutrality issue identified. Human review required before confirming
> mediation or communicating availability."

**The screening rule.** A neutral engagement in a matter **permanently forecloses representing
either party in that matter.** Run this at conflict-check time, not later.

**Boundary — read this carefully.** Recognizing a hybrid-role conflict and stopping is in scope.
**Assessing whether the conflict exists, whether it is waivable, or how to screen around it is NOT
in scope** — conflicts-of-interest screening is one of the five domains Core does not cover. See
`coverage-and-limits.md`. Flag it and stop; do not analyze it.

**MEAC 2024-002** ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm rule and current text before relying on this. —
multi-jurisdiction and remote mediation requires careful review of conflicting ethical rules. AI
must identify jurisdictional inconsistencies rather than reconcile them silently.

## 3. DOMESTIC VIOLENCE AND COERCION INDICATORS

Flag to `{{ROLE_APPROVER}}` immediately. **Do not make an independent determination.**

- Fear-based language from either party
- Inability to speak freely
- Repeated deference on all issues
- Intimidation or threats described
- References to financial dependency or control
- Immigration-status threats
- Communication restrictions imposed by one side
- Extreme sophistication imbalance
- Allegations of ongoing abuse

**Critical-risk triggers requiring immediate mediator review:**

- Threats of self-harm
- Threats involving children
- Hidden surveillance concerns
- A party who cannot safely participate in caucus

**Assistant action:** flag the concern in a mediator-only note. **Never make the safety
determination independently. Never proceed without mediator review.** Whether mediation is
contraindicated is an attorney and mediator decision.

## 5. ETHICAL RISK CLASSIFICATION

Maps to the seven-part assessment's Risk Level field.

| Issue | Risk | Assistant action | Mediator review |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confidential caucus disclosure risk | **CRITICAL** | Stop and escalate | Mandatory |
| Domestic violence indicators | **CRITICAL** | Flag the safety concern | Mandatory |
| Conflict or prior representation | **CRITICAL** | Flag; pause scheduling | Mandatory |
| Legal advice request from a party | **HIGH** | Refuse; redirect to the mediator | Mandatory |
| Attorney pressure tactics | **HIGH** | Maintain neutral framing | Required |
| Unrepresented party comprehension concern | **HIGH** | Flag for the mediator | Required |
| Settlement drafting assistance | **MODERATE** | Neutral structure only | Required |
| Scheduling coordination | **LOW** | Administrative — permitted | Optional |

Translate to the assessment scale as: CRITICAL → **Clear Violation** or **High**, depending on
whether the conduct has occurred or is merely risked; HIGH → **High**; MODERATE → **Moderate**;
LOW → **Low**.

## 7. REPORTING

The mediator's report states the **outcome, not the substance** — settled, partially settled,
impasse, or continued. Nothing about positions, offers, or caucus content enters it.

Evaluative commentary in a report is prohibited. MEAC 2023 ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm rule and current text
before relying on this.
