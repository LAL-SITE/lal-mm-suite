# Correspondence Format Standard

**Loaded by `lal-correspondence.SKILL.md` § 4.** Layout, register, and element rules for all four
lanes. Carried **in prose** because no format master ships for this document family — see § 1.

`references/conventions.md` — the conventions block bundled into every skill — governs tokens,
authority, and identity rules. Nothing here repeats them.

---

## 1. WHY THIS IS PROSE AND NOT A TEMPLATE

Pleadings, orders, and financial affidavits have a court-imposed shape that is the same for every
firm, so a format master can ship. Correspondence does not. Its shape is the buyer's brand: their
letterhead, typeface, margins, signature block, email footer.

So this file states every element, its position, and its content rule, and the buyer applies it
inside their own template. **No letterhead is generated by any skill.** § 2 states the layout the
buyer's own letterhead must satisfy; it is a specification, not a thing to render.

## 2. LETTERHEAD AND SIGNATURE-BLOCK POSITION — SPECIFICATION ONLY

### 2.1 The letter letterhead block

- **Position:** top of page one, above the date.
- **Contains:** firm name; `{{FIRM_ADDRESS}}`; `{{FIRM_PHONE}}`; `{{FIRM_EMAIL}}`. A firm website
  line is optional and buyer-supplied.
  `[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether the firm's letterhead carries a website line, and its exact form]`
- **Continuation pages:** per the buyer's own template. The source library shows no continuation
  convention, so none is specified here.
  `[CONFIRM LOCALLY: continuation-page header convention, with {{ROLE_APPROVER}}]`
- **What a skill emits instead:** a one-line position marker instructing the buyer to apply their
  own letterhead template. Never a rendered block, never a table, never an image.

### 2.2 The email signature block

An email has no letterhead. The firm's standard email signature block applies, and a skill marks
its position the same way.

Observed live email blocks carry: signer, firm name, `{{FIRM_EMAIL}}`, `{{FIRM_PHONE}}`,
`{{FIRM_ADDRESS}}`, and a confidentiality footer. Social handles and website links appeared in one
source and are **stripped** — no handle and no link ships.

### 2.3 What is never generated

Letterhead artwork. A rendered header. A brand color. A logo placeholder. A word-processor content
control. Real approved source forms carry unfilled content controls in exactly the letterhead,
addressee, delivery-method, and salutation fields — generating that region is how
`Click here to enter text.` reaches an outgoing letter.

## 3. UNIVERSAL LAYOUT

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Output format | A fully editable word-processor document, unless a different format is required after review — for example a PDF for filing with a courtesy copy to the bench |
| Typeface and size | Letters: a single serif face at 12 point. Emails may use the client's default sans face, but the stored template stays consistent with the firm's document standard |
| Spacing | Single spacing; zero paragraph spacing before and after; left alignment only; one-inch margins on all sides |
| Section headers | Bold initial caps, or bold all caps. See § 4 for which register uses which |
| Bullets | Permitted in client-facing instructional letters and emails where readability requires them, and in the enumerated factual blocks the specs define. **Not in running prose to the bench or to opposing counsel** |
| Tables | Only where a table is genuinely the clearest form — a payments ledger, a per-agreement account summary. Not for layout |
| Numbered versus bulleted | Numbered for sequence. Bulleted for inventories. **Never mixed in one block** |
| Emphasis | Bold for emphasis in body prose. Selective all caps only for a client-conduct instruction, used sparingly |
| Never present when sent | Drafting notes, bracketed instructions, competing option blocks, unresolved blanks, internal QC comments, prior-matter facts, research notes, voice notes, or a template header |

## 4. THE FOUR VOICE LANES

### 4.1 Client voice

- **Collective voice** — "we," "our firm." An individual attorney may sign, but the client
  retained the firm.
- **Warm, direct, educational.** Translate procedure into plain English without condescension.
  Assume the reader is intelligent, anxious, and short on time.
- **Answer four questions, always:** what is happening; what it means for the reader; what the
  reader must do; by when. A client letter that does not answer all four is incomplete.
- **No hedging stacks.** Be honest about real uncertainty; commit to a specific recommendation and
  a specific next step.
- **No promises.** Outcomes are goals or ranges, never guarantees. **Range outcomes; never predict
  one.** Permitted: *"you can reasonably expect a result between X and Y."* Prohibited: *"you will
  win."* The library treats prediction as a drafting defect.
- **Two delivery registers, and they are distinct house styles:**
  - *Letter format* — title case bold section headers; `Hi <first name>,` salutation.
  - *Portal or email format* — all-caps section headers; a time-of-day greeting or a first name
    followed by a comma. Never `Dear` in a client email.
- **Contractions** are live practice and are warmer than the masters. Pick one and hold it.
  `[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether client correspondence uses contractions, with {{ROLE_APPROVER}}]`
- **Person tracks the signer.** First person singular in an attorney's strategy or recommendation
  letter — *"How I read the risks," "My recommendation."* First person plural in an operational
  letter drafted at staff level — *"We send these," "We completed."*
- **Every factual bullet carries a date. Every ask carries a reason.** Whether an ask carries a
  deadline is a buyer choice; both are in live use. See the skill § 3.
- **Every ambiguous ask carries an if-it-does-not-exist branch**, so the reader can always close
  the item: *"If it's filed, send a copy. If not, tell us when you expect to file."*
- **Credit before correction.** Name what arrived and what it enabled, then name the gap. Minimize
  the miss in the header that introduces it.
- **State consequence once, briefly, then drop it.** No repetition, no escalation of tone within
  one letter. Frame the consequence as the other side's leverage, not the firm's inconvenience or
  the court's displeasure.
- **Honesty about inactivity is mandatory.** A quiet month is reported as a quiet month; never
  manufacture activity.
- **Separate advice from decision, explicitly and repeatedly** — *"This decision is yours."*
  Anti-pressure language belongs in the settlement close by default, not at the drafter's
  discretion.
- **Distinguish legal significance from personal significance** and hand the latter back.
- **Forbidden:** untranslated jargon; a judicial outcome stated as fact; a reference to a service
  the firm does not offer; any commentary on the bench; anything about opposing counsel the firm
  would not put in the file.

### 4.2 Bench voice

- **Short.** The longest body in the library is four paragraphs; most are two.
- **A logistics or transmittal instrument. Never argument.** Where a substantive point must reach
  the court it goes in a filed motion or notice.
- **Passive-to-the-court construction.** *"Enclosed for the Court's consideration and entry is…"*
  The firm does not put itself at the head of the sentence.
- **`the Court` in the third person** throughout. `Your Honor` only in the direct address of an
  optional questions line. The judge is referred to by office, not by name.
- **Neutrality is a drafting rule, not a tone preference.** A characterizing adjective is a defect.
  Issue lists carry no adjectives. Factual bullets carry dates.
- **Every request is answerable with a yes, a no, or a date.** Requests are single-issue.
- **Ask the division what it requires rather than assuming** — order format, whether a formal
  notice is needed, whether binders are accepted. This is the most repeated move in the lane.
- **`Respectfully submitted,`** in formal letters. **`Best regards,`** in judicial-assistant email.
- **The email register loosens markedly** — contractions, one-line messages, time-of-day greetings,
  plain thanks — while the substance stays purely logistical. Urgency is signalled in the subject
  line, never by pressing in the body. A brief apology for burdening the assistant's time is in
  register, used once.
- **Nothing in this lane predicts, argues, or persuades.**

### 4.3 Opposing counsel voice

- **Professionally cordial and procedurally precise.** Firm, fair, never personal. Tone never
  escalates beyond what the substance requires.
- **A one-sentence representation statement opens almost every letter**, even where counsel plainly
  knows.
- **Every substantive assertion is anchored** to a rule, a statute, or a specific document — and
  each carries ⚠️ VERIFY: confirm rule and current text before relying on this.
- **Concessions first, fully, unhedged.** Agreed-then-not-agreed order, in every negotiation letter.
- **Disagreement is a position plus a basis, never an accusation.** *"My client does not agree to
  X, as Y"* — two clauses, no adjectives.
- **Answer an aggressive position by applying its own logic symmetrically** rather than rejecting
  it. Where the other side proposes treating one category favorably to itself, reconcile both sides
  on the same rule and show the arithmetic.
- **Where the other side asserted something outside the record, request written confirmation**
  instead of arguing it.
- **Computations are shown, numbered, and attached.** State the method so the number is checkable.
- **Only one number is ever labeled `authorized`.**
- **Deadlines are concrete and carry a named consequence.** Clock times on hard deadlines. Every
  deadline is tied to a step the firm will actually take; empty deadlines train the other side to
  ignore them. Pair a demand with a fallback-identification clause — that clause is what converts
  a demand into a conferral.
- **Reciprocity is stated explicitly in courtesy letters** and tracked internally.
- **No merits argument in a scheduling or procedural letter.**
- **Frame proposals as proposals.** The word *demand* is reserved for an authorized final demand.
- **Register shifts hard for an unrepresented party** — contractions, second person, short
  sentences, no citations, no Latin, no defined terms. Even in the plain register the firm does not
  oversell: the adverse consequence of non-response sits in the same paragraph as the invitation to
  talk. The **mandatory disclaimer** closes the letter and is never omitted.

### 4.4 Third-party voice

- **Professionally neutral.** Identify the firm, the role, the matter, the purpose, and what the
  recipient is asked to do.
- **State the authority** — order, subpoena, appointment, release, or written client authorization
  — before making the ask. Across every sub-type the reason for the contact precedes the ask; the
  firm never leads with the demand.
- **Disclose only what the purpose requires.** Confirm authority exists before requesting or
  transmitting protected records. Identify the confidentiality regime that applies.
- **Four distinct registers in this lane, and they do not blend:**
  - *Court-appointed neutral, to the parties* — flat and institutional. First person singular. Every
    paragraph either grants or limits. Never *"I think"*; always *"I am required to."*
  - *Preservation notice* — dense, enumerated, cold. Bold run-in heads. Imperatives. Zero
    relationship language.
  - *Pre-service outreach to an unrepresented party* — plain-spoken, contraction-heavy, nearly
    conversational. Names the emotional stake and disclaims hostility explicitly.
  - *Instrument voice* — a release or consent is a court-caption document with decimal-numbered
    clauses and defined terms quoted on first use. Drafted to be signed, not read for persuasion.
- **Options are named, not lettered abstractly**, and each carries a one-line statement of its
  practical effect.
- **Never advise a third party.** Where a recipient may have obligations or privileges of their own,
  point them to their own counsel.
- **A neutral signs as the neutral.** The role line is load-bearing where it appears; advocate and
  neutral roles are never blended in one document.

## 5. THE LETTER SHELL — ELEMENT ORDER

### 5.1 Counsel-facing and third-party letters

1. Letterhead position marker
2. Date
3. Delivery line — stated on the face of the letter, always
4. Recipient block — name, firm, address, email; or `Custodian of Records / Legal Department`
5. `Re:` block — styling, `Case No. {{CASE_NO}}`, `{{CIRCUIT}}` circuit in and for `{{COUNTY}}`
   County, Florida, `Division {{DIVISION}}`; then a `Subject:` line naming the specific purpose.
   The `Re:` block is identification only; the purpose lives in `Subject:`
6. Salutation — see § 6
7. Representation statement — one sentence
8. Body, per the sub-type's required structure in its spec
9. The deadline or the ask
10. Close, then `Sincerely,` and the signature block
11. `Enclosures:` — enumerated by title
12. `cc:` — on the face of the letter

**Compressed caption variant.** Matter practice compresses items 5 into a single `Re:` line
carrying styling, case number, and county separated by pipes, followed by a plain-language purpose
line. Both conventions are in the harvest.
`[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether the buyer's divisions expect the expanded or compressed caption block]`

**Pre-service letters to an unrepresented party** carry a `Re:` line naming the subject of the
proceeding and **omit the case number entirely** while the matter is pre-service.

### 5.2 Formal bench letters

Same shell, with four differences:

- Delivery line defaults to a portal-submission-plus-courtesy-email form. Where a division takes
  proposed orders only through the portal, the line is modified and the assistant's email line is
  removed.
- Addressee block, in fixed order: `The Honorable {{JUDGE}}` / court name / courthouse address /
  city, state ZIP / `c/o` the judicial assistant, by role and name / that assistant's email. **Even
  a judge-addressed letter routes c/o the assistant.**
- `Re:` block on indented continuation lines.
- Close is `Respectfully submitted,`. `cc:` names opposing counsel by full name and email.

### 5.3 Client letters and emails

- *Letter:* letterhead marker, date, addressee, `Re:` line, `Hi <first name>,`, body with title
  case bold headers, close, signature block, `Attachments:` list.
- *Email:* subject line, greeting, one-to-two-sentence orientation, body with all-caps headers
  where there are multiple action items, a labeled next-steps block where anything is asked,
  close, signature-block position marker.

### 5.4 Judicial-assistant email

Subject line carrying the matter identifier and the purpose — with an all-caps urgency prefix only
where genuinely needed; time-of-day greeting; the single question or confirmation in one or two
sentences; the other side's coordination status where relevant; what the firm will do once
answered; `Best regards,` and the signature block; confidentiality footer.

### 5.5 Instrument format

A release or consent is not a letter. Court-caption format, decimal-numbered clauses in the
source's fixed order, dual signature block. No salutation, no close, no letterhead.

## 6. SALUTATIONS AND ADDRESS CONVENTIONS

|---|---|---|
| Client, letter | `Hi <first name>` | comma |
| Client, email | first name alone, or a time-of-day greeting. Never `Dear` | comma |
| Opposing counsel, named | `Dear Mr./Ms. <last name>` — never a first name | colon |

| Unrepresented adverse party | first name | comma |
| Judge | `Dear Judge <last name>` | colon |
| Judicial assistant, formal letter | `Dear Mr./Ms. <last name>` | colon |
| Judicial assistant, email | time-of-day greeting | comma or period |

Colon, never comma, in formal correspondence to a court or to counsel.

## 7. SIGNATURE BLOCKS

Signer is set by the sub-type, in the spec, deliberately. Never substitute.

| Context | Block |
|---|---|
| Attorney, counsel-facing or bench | `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}, Esq.` / `Florida Bar No. {{BAR_NO}}` / `{{FIRM_EMAIL}}` |
| Attorney, client-facing | `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}` / `{{FIRM_NAME}}` — bar number optional per firm practice |
| Staff-signed client email | `{{ROLE_DRAFTER}}` / `{{FIRM_EMAIL}}` / `{{FIRM_PHONE}}` / `{{FIRM_NAME}}` |
| Staff-signed assistant email | `{{ROLE_DRAFTER}}` / `{{FIRM_NAME}}` / `{{FIRM_EMAIL}}` / `{{FIRM_PHONE}}` / `{{FIRM_ADDRESS}}` / confidentiality footer |
| Executed-letter full block | `{{FIRM_NAME}}` / `By:` signature rule / `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}` / `Florida Bar No. {{BAR_NO}}` / `{{FIRM_ADDRESS}}` / `{{FIRM_PHONE}}` / `Service E-Mail: {{FIRM_EMAIL}}` |
| Two appearing attorneys | Both blocks, joined by `and`. The count follows who appeared, not house style |
| Court-appointed neutral | `{{ATTORNEY_NAME}}` over the role line. The role line is load-bearing |
| Instrument | Dual — consenting party above, neutral below, each over a signature rule |

**Never state a job title.** Use the role token.

**Valediction.** `Sincerely,` is the default. `Respectfully submitted,` to the bench.
`Best regards,` on staff email. `Warmly,` appears on a final settlement offer and on pre-service
outreach to an unrepresented party, where the firm signals that an adversarial document is not
personally adversarial.
`[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether a warm valediction is acceptable firm style on an adversarial letter]`

Bar number appears on counsel-facing correspondence in every template; matter practice is
inconsistent, and the two executed client letters disagree with each other — one carries a bar
number and no title, the other a title and no bar number.
`[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether client correspondence carries the bar number, a title, or both, with {{ROLE_APPROVER}}]`

## 8. SUBJECT AND `Re:` LINES

**Pattern:** `<Plain benefit, artifact, or action> — <what the reader does or gets>`. Em dash
separator. Specific and action-oriented; never `Your case` or `Update`.

- Client subject lines name the ask, not the problem.
- Whether a matter identifier may appear is contested between the masters and live practice. See
  the skill § 3.
- A settlement transmittal carries the confidential-settlement designation in the `Subject:` line,
  or as a banner above the salutation.
- A bench `Re:` block is identification; the `Subject:` line is purpose.

---

## 9. ENCLOSURES, COPIES, AND DELIVERY

- **Enclosures are enumerated by title.** Never gestured at. What the letter lists must be attached.
- **`cc:` sits on the face of the letter**, not only in the mail client. Whether the client is
  copied on opposing-counsel correspondence is a judgment call.
  `[CONFIRM LOCALLY: whether the client is copied on opposing-counsel correspondence as a matter of practice]`
- **Bench letters always copy opposing counsel**, by full name and email, and state the copy.
- **Delivery method is stated on the face of the letter, always.** A preservation notice carries a
  dual-delivery legend and a read-receipt request, with a phone follow-up if unacknowledged and the
  proof of transmission saved to the file.
- **A courtesy binder is delivered identically to opposing counsel, and the letter says so.** Tab
  numbers match the binder.

## 10. THE OPTION-BLOCK RULE

Several specs carry mutually exclusive alternatives. **Exactly one ships.** Delete the rest.

The known sets: the three professional-courtesy options; the three after-hearing circulation
states; the three phrasings of the withdrawal or non-representation mechanic — and the corpus
contains no rule for choosing among that third set, because which instrument is correct depends on
posture and division.
`[CONFIRM LOCALLY: motion versus notice for ending representation, by division and case posture]`

Shipping a letter with competing option blocks is named in the source as the most common formatting
error in the library.

## 11. PRE-RELEASE FORMAT CHECK

A short list; the full gate is `qc-checklist.md`.

- Recipient identified by full legal name and current address or email.
- Matter identified by styling, case number, and division where applicable. Date current.
- Subject or `Re:` line specific and accurate.
- All tokens resolved; no bracketed instruction remains; no `Click here to enter text.`; no
  placeholder X's.
- Attachments enclosed and enumerated. `cc:` accurate and complete.
- Exactly one option block survives.
- Signature block matches the signer the spec specifies.
- Any internal block stripped.
- Register consistent — header style, person, and contraction use do not switch mid-document.
- Inapplicable warning clauses pruned.
